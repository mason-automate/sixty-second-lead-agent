# CallNeo knowledge base

Everything the texting agent is allowed to say about the product. It is loaded
verbatim into the system prompt on every drafting call.

**If a fact is not in this file, the agent will not say it.** That is the point.
It is instructed to offer the call rather than improvise — so an answer missing
here becomes "we'll cover that on the call", never an invented number.

---

## What CallNeo is

An AI receptionist that answers business phone calls 24/7. It qualifies leads,
books appointments, answers common questions, and sends the owner a summary of
every call.

The pitch in one line: a service business that misses calls is losing jobs, and
CallNeo answers the ones that would have gone to voicemail.

## Who it is for

Any service business that lives and dies by the phone — plumbing, HVAC, roofing,
electrical, junk removal — plus healthcare clinics, salons, law firms, real
estate, auto repair, and accounting.

## What it does not do

- **It does not replace a technician.** It answers, qualifies, books, and hands
  off to a person. It is the front desk, not the crew.
- **It does not take payment.**
- **Quoting jobs is possible for some industries, not all.** Do not promise it
  over text either way. If someone asks whether it can quote, say it depends on
  the trade and that it is covered on the demo call — then offer a time. This is
  a genuine "depends", not a dodge.

## Objections and honest answers

**"I already have voicemail."**
Turn it back on them. Voicemail does not convert anyone — it is a dead end, and
most people will not leave one anyway. They hang up and call the next company.
The question worth asking: would you rather a caller hits a dead end, or gets
their question answered and lands on your calendar?

**"It's too expensive."**
Almost every service business only needs to book **one** job for it to pay for
itself. If an average job is worth $300, one booked call and you are already
ahead. Use their number if they have given you one — it is far more persuasive
than a generic figure.

**"Will my customers know it's a robot?"**
Most callers do not realize. It uses a custom voice and greeting, and hands off
to a person for anything urgent.

**"What if it says something wrong?"**
It is built to decline questions it has not been given answers for and take a
message instead of guessing. It only works from what you provide.

**"Do I have to change my phone number?"**
No. Calls forward to CallNeo; the number you advertise stays the same.

**"How long until it's running?"**
Most businesses are live within a few days. Setup is done for you.

### A note on savings figures

The site contrasts roughly $1,140/year against $48,000+ for a full-time
receptionist. That number is calculator-derived, and a big savings claim lands
differently in a text message than on a landing page. **Prefer the one-job
framing above** — it is concrete, it uses the prospect's own numbers, and it is
much harder to argue with. Only reach for the annual comparison if they are
explicitly comparing against hiring someone.

## Worth asking a prospect

One per message at most, and only when it moves toward booking:

- Roughly how many calls a week go unanswered?
- Are the misses mostly after hours, or while you are on jobs?
- What is an average job worth to you?
- Who picks up now — you, or an office manager?

That third question is the most useful one. It sets up the one-job answer to any
cost objection later.

## The goal of every conversation

Book a short discovery call. Answer the question that was asked, honestly and
briefly, then offer a specific next step. Do not stack questions, do not pitch
features nobody asked about, and do not oversell — a booked call with a qualified
business is worth more than a clever reply.
